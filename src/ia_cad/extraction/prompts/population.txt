TASK: Extract population/sample information from this scientific article for the IADAS knowledge graph.

EXTRACTION RULES:
1. Extract ONLY the predicates listed above — no other predicates.
2. Only extract values explicitly stated or clearly implied by the text. Never invent.
3. iadas:gender — the overall gender composition of the sample. Use ONE of the ALLOWED VALUES
   above. Key signals:
   - "Female"  : "female participants", "women", "girls", or a sample described as 100% one sex.
   - "Male"    : "male participants", "men", "boys", or a sample described as 100% one sex.
   - "Mixed"   : both sexes present in any split (e.g. "124 males, 148 females") — use Mixed
                 regardless of how unequal the split is, do NOT pick the majority sex.
   Omit only if the text gives no indication of sex composition at all.
4. iadas:sampleSize — integer. The TOTAL number of participants recruited/included in the study
   (not the number in a specific sub-analysis — that is iadas:sampleSizeMobilized, extracted
   elsewhere). Use the number BEFORE any exclusions described later, if both are given. When
   several counts appear (recruited / eligible / final), prefer the one explicitly framed as the
   total sample (e.g. "A total of N participants...", "N athletes took part"), not a per-group count.
5. iadas:inclusionCriteria — the eligibility criteria stated for participation (age range, sport
   experience, health status, competition level, etc.), as a short free-text summary. Omit if no
   explicit inclusion criteria are stated.
6. iadas:population — how the participants are characterized overall. Prefer one of the ALLOWED
   VALUES above ("Athletes" or "Students") when it clearly fits; if the sample does not clearly
   fit either (e.g. general population, non-athlete non-student adults, a study comparing
   athletes to non-athletes), write the term used in the text instead of forcing a bad fit
   (e.g. "Athletes and non-athletes", "General population").
   ⚠ NEVER leave this empty if the article describes the sample at all — omitting is only for
   when the text gives no population information whatsoever, not when it doesn't fit the two
   allowed values.
   ⚠ "Students" vs "Athletes" — this is the most common confusion for this field. A university
   or school named only as the RECRUITMENT CHANNEL or DATA-COLLECTION SITE is NOT enough for
   "Students": if the participants are recruited there BECAUSE they practice a sport (student
   athletes, a sport-science/PE student sample defined by their sport practice), the population
   is "Athletes". Use "Students" only when the sample is framed as students/pupils in general,
   with no sport-practice criterion defining who was recruited.
7. iadas:hasSubgroup — a free-text description of subgroups within the sample, if the article
   breaks the sample down (e.g. by gender counts, by sport, by competition level, by site).
   Trigger: a sentence or small table giving a per-subgroup count ("n=..." repeated for several
   groups), e.g. "161 Males 160 Females" or "MDD n=60, MLD n=156, WMD n=61, WLD n=123". Combine
   multiple crossed factors (e.g. distance x sex) into ONE free-text description rather than
   inventing separate predicates. Omit if the sample is not described in subgroups.
8. iadas:sportingPopulation — a free-text characterization of the sporting population specifically
   (e.g. "Regular sport practitioners", "Elite competitive athletes", "Recreational exercisers",
   or a competitive-tier breakdown such as "87.67% varsity, 6.85% recreational, 5.48% club").
   Trigger: look in Participants/Method for how the article itself qualifies the athletes'
   competitive level or practice regularity, beyond the broad iadas:population category above.
   Distinct from iadas:population — this is a more specific sporting descriptor. Omit if the
   article gives no such qualifier beyond the broad category.
