EXTRACTION RULES:
1. Extract ONLY the predicates listed above — no other predicates.
2. Only extract values explicitly stated or clearly implied by the text. Never invent.
3. iadas:country — the country where the study / data collection took place. This is usually
   stated in the Method/Participants section (e.g. "participants were recruited in Croatia",
   "conducted in the United States", "N athletes from Belgian clubs"). Prefer the exact wording
   from the ALLOWED VALUES list above when the article's country matches one (or a close variant,
   e.g. "USA"/"US" -> "United States of America", "UK" -> "United Kingdom").
   Do NOT use the country of the journal's publisher, or of a co-author's affiliation if that
   author did not collect the data — only the country of the STUDY POPULATION / data collection.
   If participants were recruited from MULTIPLE countries, write them together as they appear in
   the text (e.g. "New Zealand Australia") rather than picking only one.
   Omit if the country cannot be determined from the text.
4. iadas:studyType — the methodological design of the study. Use ONE of the ALLOWED VALUES above:
   - "Cross-sectional studies": data collected at a single time point (most common — the DEFAULT
     whenever the text does not describe repeated measurements over time, even if the article
     never uses the literal word "cross-sectional").
   - "Longitudinal": participants followed and measured at multiple time points over time,
     without necessarily being framed as a formal cohort. Key signals: "at three time points",
     "6 months apart", "follow-up assessment", "T1/T2/T3".
   - "Cohort studies": a defined group (cohort) followed over time, often with explicit cohort
     terminology or comparison between exposed/unexposed groups.
   - "Prospective studies": data collection planned and carried out forward in time from the
     study's start (explicitly described as "prospective").
   - "Case study": a single participant or a very small number of individually described cases.
   When both "Longitudinal" and "Cohort studies" could apply, prefer whichever term the article
   itself uses to describe its own design.
   ⚠ Do NOT omit this field just because the design isn't named explicitly. A Method section
   describing ONE round of questionnaires/measurements with no follow-up or repeated time point
   IS a cross-sectional design — extract "Cross-sectional studies" in that case. Only omit when
   the text genuinely gives no basis to judge (extremely rare — e.g. a pure literature review
   with no primary data collection at all).
